The paper compares 5%, 45% and 85% of the patterns; these are those three with 15% added, so the step from 5% to 45% is not a jump. Each panel is the ensemble-mean path of every layer over the whole run, drawn exactly as Figure 2 draws a mean. Compression happens in all four — what changes is whether the layers keep their label information while it does.

Final mean accuracy after 9,000 epochs, per panel.

What the panels are for. The paper's claim here is not that compression needs data — it happens in every panel — but that what compression costs depends on how much data there is. With few samples the deep layers lose label information as they compress, which is overfitting the sample rather than the rule; with most of the data they compress and keep it. On the theoretical IB bound. The plan for this page called for overlaying the information-bottleneck bound curve. Computing it means solving the IB self-consistent equations for this joint distribution, which the Phase 1 pipeline does not do, so the curve is not drawn rather than drawn by eye.
